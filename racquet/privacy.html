Privacy policy

Racquet for iOS · Updated October 9, 2026

This policy covers the Racquet iOS app maintained by Kosta Karathanasopoulos. The Chrome extension has a separate policy.

Your game

Solo play runs on your device. Your best score, saved rally, multiplayer nickname and whether you have seen the help screen are stored locally. The app also keeps local settings used to manage ads. Some preferences are copied into iOS app storage so they survive a restart of the game view. Racquet has no account or cloud score service.

Touch input controls the racquet. The app uses screen dimensions and your reduced-motion preference for display. It does not request access to your camera, microphone, contacts or precise location. Game sounds are generated on the device.

Advertising

The iOS app uses Google AdMob to display ads around breaks in play. Ads need an internet connection. The game remains playable when an ad is unavailable.

Racquet requests non-personalized ads. It does not request permission to access Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA), and it disables AdMob's publisher first-party identifier and ads personalization. This does not mean that ads process no data.

Google's advertising SDK may process your IP address, which can indicate a general location; app or device identifiers other than IDFA; information about ads shown and interactions with them; and crash, performance and other diagnostic information. Google uses this information to deliver ads, measure performance, prevent fraud and operate its services. Racquet does not send your solo score, saved rally or multiplayer nickname to AdMob.

Google's User Messaging Platform manages applicable privacy notices and choices. The app checks whether ads may be requested before loading them. Where required, you can reopen Privacy choices in the app to review or change your selections. Non-personalized ads may still use device storage for purposes such as measurement and frequency limits. Your choices can affect the ads available to show.

Learn more in Google's Privacy Policy, how Google uses information from apps that use its services, and AdMob's data disclosure. Google controls its own service retention periods.

Optional multiplayer

Multiplayer starts when you choose it and go online with a nickname. An invitation must be accepted before a match starts. Nicknames are not verified identities; use one that does not contain personal information.

  • PeerServer Cloud at 0.peerjs.com receives nicknames, connection identifiers and connection setup messages to find the other player. Like other internet services, it receives your IP address.
  • Google's STUN service helps establish a direct connection. Network addresses can be visible to these services and the other player.
  • Players exchange nicknames, invitations, racquet movement, ball and match state, scores, timing and connection status over an encrypted WebRTC connection.

The developer does not operate a server that stores matches. There is no chat, voice or video feature. Match state stays in memory for the session, and multiplayer results do not change your solo best score. Leaving multiplayer ends the connection. Service providers control their operational logs. See PeerServer Cloud and Google's Privacy Policy.

Keeping or deleting data

Local game information stays in the app until replaced or the app's data is deleted. Deleting the app can erase your score and preferences; offloading the app may retain its data. Racquet does not provide an online score recovery service. iOS backup settings may also affect whether app data is retained in a device backup.

For questions or requests about information you sent directly to the developer, email kostakarathanasopoulos@gmail.com. Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal information, object to certain processing, or withdraw consent. For information held by Google or another service, use that service's privacy controls or contact process.

Support and this website

If you email support, the developer receives your email address and message and uses them to answer your request. Avoid sending sensitive information. Support messages are kept as needed to handle the issue and related follow-up. GitHub issues are public and subject to GitHub's Privacy Statement. This site is hosted by GitHub Pages, which receives visitor network information. The site has no advertising or analytics scripts.

Children and changes

Do not send children's personal information through multiplayer nicknames or support. Contact the developer if you believe a child has provided personal information. Updated practices will be reflected here with a new date, along with any notice or consent required for the change.
